# Project Profitability Analysis

## Project Purpose
Arbit is a modular triangular arbitrage trading platform that scans cryptocurrency exchanges for three-leg price dislocations and can optionally execute the full trade cycle across supported venues such as Alpaca and Kraken via a Typer CLI, Prometheus metrics, and SQLite persistence.【F:README.md†L1-L59】【F:docs/WARP.md†L8-L25】 The system already exposes both read-only monitoring (`fitness`) and live trading commands, underlining its ambition to capture micro-inefficiencies automatically.【F:README.md†L52-L59】【F:docs/WARP.md†L18-L21】

## Profitability Outlook
Triangular arbitrage margins are inherently thin, requiring low-latency market data, precise fee and slippage accounting, and high operational reliability to consistently realize profits. Today, Arbit samples markets roughly once per second with CCXT rate limits and relies on simplified top-of-book limit orders, leaving it structurally slower than professional competitors and vulnerable to stale quotes.【F:docs/WARP.md†L22-L55】 In addition, the project explicitly flags that fees, slippage, and partial fills are not fully modeled, making its current net-edge estimates optimistic versus real executions.【F:README.md†L7-L14】【F:docs/WARP.md†L38-L55】 Given those gaps and the reality that the codebase is “not ready for production trading,” the short-term probability of sustainable profitability is low without significant investment in execution safety, data quality, and risk management.【F:README.md†L7-L15】【F:docs/WARP.md†L135-L149】

## Key Barriers to Profitability
1. **Incomplete execution economics** – Profit calculations omit venue fees, slippage, and partial fill handling, so trade sizing and thresholds are systematically overstated relative to real-world fills.【F:README.md†L7-L14】【F:docs/WARP.md†L38-L55】  
2. **Latency and market access constraints** – The current 1-second polling loop with CCXT rate limiting and limited exchange coverage (Alpaca, Kraken) reduces the odds of capturing fleeting arbitrage windows and increases adverse-selection risk.【F:docs/WARP.md†L12-L25】  
3. **Missing risk controls and resilience** – The live path lacks IOC enforcement, explicit dry-run toggles, slippage limits, and kill switches, making it difficult to contain losses or halt trading during incidents.【F:docs/WARP.md†L151-L173】  
4. **Operational readiness gaps** – Phase II roadmap items such as multi-symbol rotation, fee modeling, monitoring, and Dockerized production workflows remain unchecked, reflecting the engineering effort still required before deploying capital confidently.【F:docs/WARP.md†L135-L149】  
5. **Regulatory and capital considerations** – Running live arbitrage demands compliant API access, exchange-specific onboarding, and sufficient capital buffers to survive volatility; these elements are outside the repository’s scope and introduce external friction that can delay profitability despite working code.【F:README.md†L16-L49】

## Feasibility of Overcoming Barriers
- **Execution economics** – Incorporating taker fees, slippage models, and partial-fill accounting is feasible but requires engine refactors, better market depth tracking, and regression tests to ensure accuracy. The roadmap already calls for these upgrades, yet they represent non-trivial quantitative engineering work before trading thresholds become trustworthy.【F:docs/WARP.md†L38-L55】【F:docs/ROADMAP_PHASE_II.md†L36-L69】  
- **Latency and venue expansion** – Achieving competitive latency would entail WebSocket market data, faster order routing, and potentially colocated infrastructure. Phase II deliverables describe the necessary WebSocket plumbing and inventory rotation, indicating technical feasibility, but implementation complexity and exchange fees may keep Arbit behind specialized HFT firms even after completion.【F:docs/ROADMAP_PHASE_II.md†L1-L58】  
- **Risk controls and resilience** – Features like IOC orders, circuit breakers, and kill switches are standard in professional trading stacks and appear on the roadmap; building and validating them is realistic but will demand disciplined testing and simulation to avoid unintended consequences.【F:docs/WARP.md†L167-L173】【F:docs/ROADMAP_PHASE_II.md†L60-L120】  
- **Operational readiness** – Hardening metrics, persistence, configuration, and CI/CD can be addressed incrementally, yet the breadth of outstanding work implies a multi-phase effort before the platform supports unattended operations, delaying time-to-profit.【F:docs/ROADMAP_PHASE_II.md†L58-L134】  
- **Regulatory/capital hurdles** – These external barriers depend on organizational resources, compliance policies, and funding; while solvable, they may outweigh the attainable edge if the technical stack cannot secure reliable profits quickly.【F:README.md†L16-L49】

Overall, the engineering upgrades are technically achievable, but they require substantial time, expertise, and potentially costly market access investments. Even with those improvements, the competitive landscape for triangular arbitrage (where market makers and exchanges themselves pursue similar edges) makes it uncertain that Arbit can sustain positive PnL without ongoing maintenance and capital expenditure.

## Alternative or Complementary Profit Strategies
- **Leverage the platform as a monitoring and analytics tool** – Positioning Arbit as a read-only opportunity scanner with Prometheus metrics, education, or paid alerts could generate subscription-style revenue without the risk of executing trades, while capitalizing on existing CLI and metrics infrastructure.【F:README.md†L52-L59】【F:docs/WARP.md†L18-L25】  
- **Expand DeFi yield tooling** – The repository already contains beta yield commands and Aave integration hooks, suggesting a potential pivot toward stablecoin treasury automation or yield-optimization services where execution latency is less critical than risk management and compliance.【F:README.md†L97-L100】【F:docs/WARP.md†L89-L125】  
- **Offer managed services or consulting** – Documented gaps in production readiness and safety could be reframed as consulting opportunities, helping clients adapt Arbit’s codebase while charging for customization, integration, or operational support.【F:docs/WARP.md†L135-L173】  

## Recommendation
Given the current state, pursuing live triangular arbitrage profits with Arbit is unlikely to be immediately profitable and would require completing a substantial portion of the Phase II roadmap—including fee-aware execution, WebSocket data, and comprehensive risk controls—before risking capital.【F:docs/WARP.md†L135-L173】【F:docs/ROADMAP_PHASE_II.md†L1-L120】 Management should evaluate whether the organization possesses the expertise and budget for that investment; otherwise, redirecting effort toward lower-risk revenue streams (analytics, tooling, or DeFi services) may deliver faster, more predictable returns while the core arbitrage engine matures.【F:README.md†L52-L100】【F:docs/WARP.md†L18-L125】
